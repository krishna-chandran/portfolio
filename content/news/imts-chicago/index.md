CHICAGO — In September 2018 the Mazak Spindle AI Health Monitor was shown at the International Manufacturing Technology Show. The names on the banner were Dr. Hossein Davari, Dr. Vibhor Pandhare and Krishnakumar Chandran, representing Mazak and the Center for Intelligent Maintenance Systems at the University of Cincinnati.
<!--more-->

{{< well kind="lead" prompt="Wide, 16:9: IMTS-style show booth, a CNC and a small UI, documentary, no readable leads." caption="Chicago, 2018. A trade floor, not a seminar room." credit="Staff" />}}

IMTS is where machine-tool houses put metal in front of buyers. The monitor was not a poster. It was a health score from 0 to 100, trained on vibration and current from a Mazak CNC — new, dry, imbalanced, broken — classified with FFT features, principal-component reduction and logistic regression, and shown on a Flask web app. Below 50 meant service. Below 30, the machine was not to be used. A shop that owns a machine in the hundred-thousand to ten-million range stops the line for a checklist; the bid was to stop when the signal said so.

Mazak implemented the work in its CNC machines. The university [wrote the story up in January 2019](https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2019/01/n2063491.html). That article is the public clip. The demonstration is the date the research left the lab and stood under lights.
