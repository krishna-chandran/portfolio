UNITED STATES — From 2019 to 2021 Krishnakumar Chandran sat with Mercedes-Benz Financial Services to deploy ignio AIOps in the company’s data center. MBFS is the exclusive financial engine behind Mercedes-Benz USA, the captive that finances the cars. The outcome on the file is a walk from a person-first incident queue toward a machine-first delivery model — MFDM, in the language TCS used for that transformation.

He was then TCS’s product architect for ignio. The collaboration was the customer engagement the role required: hear the operational challenge, study the servers, network devices and business applications already in the estate, design the integration, and stay while the product went live. Event triage, incident prediction and automatic resolution — the ignio capabilities from the Chennai years — had to land on a live data center, not a lab.

Belk, Toyota Motors North America and Astellas sat in the same years, across retail, automobiles and pharmaceuticals. The public claim for ignio’s footprint — more than 260 Fortune 500 and Global 2000 corporations, most of them in the United States — is the product’s, not a count of personal installs. The MBFS engagement is the one named in the career file as a complete digitalization of IT servicing.
