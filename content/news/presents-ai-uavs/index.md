VELLORE — On 1 August 2013 Krishnakumar Chandran and Vijay S presented “Application of Artificial Intelligence in Unmanned Aerial Vehicles” at a national seminar at C. Abdul Hakeem College of Engineering and Technology.

He was still an undergraduate in aeronautical engineering at Rajalakshmi. The paper was not a product. It was the first public argument that the same student who sat flight dynamics could also sit a learning algorithm.

The venue was a national seminar, not a trade show. The authors were two. The date is on the program. What followed — ignio in a data center, a Mazak spindle, an airport tractor, a farm fleet — is easier to file if this column is in the book.
