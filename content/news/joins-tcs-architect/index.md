FARMINGTON HILLS, Mich. — In July 2019 Krishnakumar Chandran rejoined Tata Consultancy Services as a product architect. The appointment ran through September 2021. The products were ignio AIOps and ignio AI Digital Workspace — and, in the family, ignio Butterfly. The brief was production deployment in on-prem data centers, not a slide. He had the Chennai years and a master’s in aerospace behind him. The job was to sit with a client estate and walk it from a ticket queue toward a machine-first model.
<!--more-->

The architect’s card is longer than an integration list. He was the ignio product architect for a customer environment: understand the problem and the opportunity, then design, configure and deploy the solution on ignio. That meant reading the existing landscape for redundant or ineffective systems, writing an end-to-end design for information and operations systems, and staying with the client long enough to get organizational commitment and to choose the technologies the plan actually required.

He coordinated with offshore development teams on priorities, scope and delivery dates. He wrote data-migration and system-integration strategies. He watched performance through go-live and through change management. He also sat the cognitive work: AI and ML use cases on ignio, not only connectors.

The connectors still had to exist. He integrated the product with the tools a client already owned — ServiceNow, SolarWinds, AppDynamics, Office 365, Active Directory, email and CyberArk. On Veeva Vault he automated user access and put chat-based ticket creation in front of the request. He built a log stream on Apache Kafka, Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana and Beats. That Kafka–ELK path cut debugging time by 90 percent. ignio went out in high availability, Ansible-driven. Self-healing closed Linux, database and application alerts in seconds.

Mercedes-Benz Financial Services, Belk, Toyota Motors North America and Astellas sat among the accounts — automobiles, retail, pharmaceuticals. The MBFS engagement is the one on the file as a complete walk from manual-first incident response to a machine-first delivery model. The ignio product’s footprint is more than 260 Fortune 500 and Global 2000 shops; his piece was the architect who sat with the client, and the network and Windows Server automation from the Chennai years that form part of that product’s backbone.
