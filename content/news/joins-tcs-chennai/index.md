CHENNAI — In September 2014 Krishnakumar Chandran joined Tata Consultancy Services as a software developer. The product was ignio AIOps, Digitate’s platform for data-center operations. The appointment ran through July 2017. He had a first degree in aeronautical engineering and a Tejas paper behind him. The new brief was a closet that should not need a person at two in the morning.
<!--more-->

{{< well kind="lead" prompt="Wide, 16:9, Chennai office night: cubicles, network diagrams on a whiteboard, a single desk lamp, documentary." caption="TCS, Chennai. Network automation and ignio service operations." credit="Staff" />}}

ignio’s pitch was already an AI digital platform: network automation, then the quieter work of event triage, incident prediction and automatic resolution. He wrote the scripts and the service operations that made that pitch a night list a machine could run.

About 160 ignio scripts covered some 90 percent of the network-administration tasks the shop still did by hand. The devices were the ones a data center actually owns: L2 and L3 switches, firewalls, load balancers, F5 and Infoblox. Service Operations on ignio meant those boxes could be handled without walking the closet. The languages were Python, PowerShell, Groovy and bash. The estate was Windows and Linux. PostgreSQL held what needed holding.

The network and Windows Server automation modules from this line later sat in the backbone of a product deployed at more than 260 Fortune 500 and Global 2000 corporations. That footprint is the product’s, not a count of personal installs. His piece was the modules, and the habit of watching an estate until the ticket could close itself.

In 2016 the same product went to Nielsen Media India for NetBackup automation and backup analytics. About half of that shop’s manual reporting and IT-operations time went away. TCS’s Ignite training and continuous-learning programs sat beside the undergraduate file; the multidisciplinary mix is how a structures student became a person who could automate a switch.

The habit — observe the estate, automate the night list — is the one he later took to a spindle, a tractor and a farm.
