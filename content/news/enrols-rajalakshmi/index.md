CHENNAI — In August 2010 Krishnakumar Chandran, from Erode, Tamil Nadu, enrolled for a Bachelor of Engineering in aeronautical engineering at Rajalakshmi Engineering College, affiliated to Anna University. The course ran through April 2014. The GPA was 7.6 on 10. The syllabus was still an airplane.

He had finished school at Navarasam Matriculation Higher Secondary School in Erode. The brief he gave himself was aerospace, not a desk job. The sequence he sat was the aeronautical one: mechanics, flight dynamics, aerodynamics, computational fluid dynamics, structures, composites, propulsion and avionics.

EDS Technologies, the largest PLM house in India and a platinum partner of Dassault Systèmes, certified him on CATIA V5 primer and advanced courses — parts, surfaces and assemblies, the language used to design physical machines. Candidates sat a timed, performance-based exam. The certificate is the one the global aerospace industry still recognises as shop language.

In 2013 he would take that training into a national seminar on AI in unmanned aerial vehicles. In 2014 the same college year produced a structural project at Hindustan Aeronautics on the Tejas slat actuator. Software came after the degree, not instead of it.
